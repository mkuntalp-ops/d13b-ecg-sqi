Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, "Evaluating LiquidAI d1-3B for ECG usability screening: a patient-independent, multi-dataset benchmark", as a Research paper in Physiological Measurement.

Signal-quality assessment is the first step of most automated ECG analysis pipelines, and key methodological work on ECG signal quality indices, including detector-agreement bSQI and SQI fusion, was published in this journal. The study evaluates LiquidAI d1-3B, an open vision-language decision model, for ECG usability screening: zero-shot, as intended, and through a linear probe on its frozen representation. It also asks a practical benchmarking question: does a model's ranking under patient-independent in-domain evaluation tell a practitioner which model will work on another device and label definition?

Eleven approaches were developed on BUT QDB with subject-grouped cross-validation and evaluated without change on the CinC 2011 and CinC 2017 datasets. They comprise signal-quality-index classifiers including a tabular foundation model, raw-signal and image convolutional networks, a multimodal network, a rule-based heuristic, a zero-shot vision-language model, and linear probes on two frozen foundation models; seven of them were additionally evaluated under paired electrode-motion noise stress using NSTDB. The analyses include paired AUROC comparisons with multiplicity control, leave-one-dataset-out training repeated over five seeds, operating points re-set on small labelled target samples, and sensitivity analyses under the official BUT QDB protocol.

The zero-shot decisions of d1-3B were weak, but a linear probe on its frozen image tokens was the only approach that was best or not significantly behind the best in every evaluation set, so the value of this general-purpose model lies in its representation rather than its decisions. Ten of the eleven approaches did not differ significantly from the best in-domain model, yet their external rankings disagreed. Multi-source training helped some architectures and harmed others. At realistic prevalence, thresholds re-set on 100–200 labelled recordings controlled false rejections but left sensitivity poorly determined, and several trained models scored completely flat leads as usable. The practical message is that ECG quality models need external evaluation on data whose labels match the intended use, local calibration with enough positive examples, and an explicit check for constant signals.

The later analyses were specified in a dated analysis plan before they were run, and post hoc analyses are labelled as such. The supplementary material provides full feature definitions, architectures and the heuristic formula, confidence intervals for all models, predictor distributions by dataset, and a completed TRIPOD+AI checklist.

For transparency, I note that a separate manuscript, "Teacher–Student Distillation of TabPFN for Retrospective Two-Lead ECG Features: Procedure-Matched Evaluation and Cohort-Composition Sensitivity" (PMEA-107866), is currently under consideration at Physiological Measurement. That study examines procedure-matched knowledge distillation and label-smoothing controls for retrospective ECG classification, primarily using PTB-XL-derived lead I/II features. The present submission addresses a different research question, ECG usability screening and cross-dataset transfer, using BUT QDB, the CinC 2011 and 2017 datasets and NSTDB noise-stress experiments. Both studies use TabPFN, but in different roles: as the teacher model for distillation in the earlier manuscript, and as one of eleven compared approaches here. The manuscripts share no datasets, analyses or results, and they have distinct prediction targets, principal analyses and conclusions.
