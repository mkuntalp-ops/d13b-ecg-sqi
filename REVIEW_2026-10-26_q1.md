## 9. Reframing around d1-3B (2026-10-26, author request)

The author noted that the study's primary aim was to evaluate the decision model LiquidAI d1-3B against classical and task-specific methods. The manuscript was reframed accordingly, without changing any data or number.

**New title.** "LiquidAI d1-3B for ECG usability screening: weak zero-shot decisions but the most consistently transferring frozen representation in a patient-independent, multi-dataset benchmark". The model is named, so the title does not suggest that a new model was developed.

**What changed.**
- Abstract: 248 words.
- Introduction: the aim statement.
- Discussion: the opening paragraph.
- Conclusions.
- Cover letter.
- TRIPOD item 1.

**Main message.** The zero-shot decisions are inadequate, but the frozen representation transferred most consistently.

**Limitations.** These now state that:
- the d1-3B probe was introduced in an earlier revision, after the zero-shot results were known;
- the probe needs labelled data and carries the full model's cost (891 ms per window).

**Corrections made during this change.**
- "Flagged every CinC 2011 recording" was corrected to 564 of 565 (checked).
- "Best on CinC 2017" was made precise: highest AUROC, but not significantly higher than the HuBERT-ECG probe and the heuristic.

The author then asked for a shorter title, which was set to "LiquidAI d1-3B for ECG usability screening". The two-part finding (weak zero-shot decisions, best-transferring probe) is now carried by the abstract.
On reflection the short title read as an endorsement and hid the benchmark design, so the final title is "Evaluating LiquidAI d1-3B for ECG usability screening: a patient-independent, multi-dataset benchmark" (13 words).
