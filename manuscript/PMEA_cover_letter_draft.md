Dear Editor,

Please consider my manuscript, "Evaluating LiquidAI d1-3B for ECG usability screening: a patient-independent, multi-dataset benchmark", for publication as a Research paper in Physiological Measurement.

This study examines whether a general-purpose vision-language model can help assess ECG signal usability across different datasets. Its zero-shot decisions were limited, but a supervised probe on its frozen representation performed competitively against established approaches. The findings also show that strong performance on development data does not necessarily translate into reliable performance on new recordings.

I believe the manuscript is well suited to Physiological Measurement because it addresses a practical problem in physiological signal analysis: how to evaluate and select ECG quality models for use beyond their development setting. The contribution is a comparative assessment of model transfer and its limitations, rather than a claim of a universally superior algorithm.

For transparency, another manuscript of mine is currently under consideration at Physiological Measurement (PMEA-107866). It concerns TabPFN-based knowledge distillation for retrospective ECG classification using PTB-XL features. The present submission addresses ECG usability and cross-dataset transfer using different datasets, with distinct analyses and conclusions.

This manuscript is original, has not been published previously and is not under consideration elsewhere. The manuscript includes disclosures on generative-AI assistance, ethics, funding and competing interests, together with data and code availability information.

Thank you for considering my submission.
